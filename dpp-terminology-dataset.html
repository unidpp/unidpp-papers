A terminology dataset for the Digital Product Passport

Announcing the UniDPP terminology dataset: a machine-readable, glossarist-managed concept collection extracted verbatim from the EN 18216–18246 series, CWA 18291 and IDTA 01001 — every definition carrying its clause-level citation, every definitional conflict recorded rather than silently merged — offered as the single source of truth for the vocabulary part of AWI ISO 25534-1, with the new-work-item proposal (ISO Form 4) attached under CalConnect attribution.

Re-rendered 2026-09-20 against UniDPP specification 762710c (the correlation doctrine of clause 6.3 k joined the framework after the inaugural offering)
CalConnect — dataset steward in the UniDPP framework (multi-body stewardship)
2026-09-07 · Accompanies ISO Form 4 (NWIP-1, Part 1 Vocabulary) · deployed at glossarist.org/dpp

01 · AnnouncementWhat the dataset is

The UniDPP terminology dataset is a working dataset of Digital Product Passport concepts, stewarded under CalConnect-originated multi-body stewardship and published as a Glossarist Concept Browser edition. It is seeded from the converted corpus described in paper 1 of this series — the eight ENs of the 18216–18246 series, two CWAs, 77 IDTA specifications, AWI ISO 25534-1 and the JTC 5 plenary document set — and follows the architecture of the glossarist cie-eilv dataset, the proven pattern for standards-body terminology publishing in this toolchain. The deploy target is glossarist.org/dpp.

The verified harvest stands at 141 extracted entries — 82 from the EN series, 30 from CWA 18291, and 29 from IDTA 01001 — reconciled by the glossarist audit into 146 verified concepts: 123 harvested concepts plus 23 proposed framework terms, organized into eight sections fixed in the dataset register: digital product passport; identity and carriers; actors and issuance; lifecycle and events; trust and verification; semantics and profiles; measurement; and framework terms. Paper 4 of this series records the dataset among the built assets of the reference implementation ("terminology dataset (multilingual) — Glossarist dataset [built]").

The eighth section, framework terms, is where the dataset absorbs the framework's own vocabulary rather than the standards corpus: twenty-three proposed concepts — profile, characteristic profile, projection, lens, stamp, transformation event, derived passport, Tier-A payload, passive twin, active twin, custody transfer, dormant identifier — each defined from the framework documents and typed proposed rather than harvested. The framework's later vocabulary — blind edge, escrowed disclosure, proof of binding, enumeration resistance, predicate-based recall, trigger predicate, installation, recoverability, harvested part, truth mode, as-of state — enters the same section through the same registered workflow as the concepts stabilize.

The dataset exists because the terminology workstream is the first of the consortium's workstreams to reach a deliverable-ready state: the ISO–CEN definitional conflict over the passport concept itself is on the table, the vocabulary Part 1 of 25534-1 is an approved work item in search of a drafting basis, and no machine-readable, citation-complete term base exists across the EN series, the CWAs and the IDTA specifications. This dataset is that basis, and this paper is its formal announcement to JTC 5.

02 · MethodVerbatim harvest with clause citations

Three rules governed the harvest, and all three are enforced by the data model rather than by editorial discipline:

  • Definitions are verbatim. A definition is copied exactly as the cited source states it, never rewritten, never reconciled. If two sources disagree, both texts survive, each with its citation.
  • Every source carries its clause. A term's provenance is recorded as reference plus clause — EN 18223:2026, 3.4, not "the EN series" — so any entry can be checked against its authority in one lookup.
  • Relationships between sources are typed. Each source entry carries a type: authoritative (the definition's home), authoritatives-equivalent (a second authority stating the same intension), conflicting (a divergence recorded for harmonization, with a modification note), or lineage (a source in the term's history, without a clause).

Where a term inherits from the general standards vocabulary, the dataset cites the upstream standard rather than paraphrasing it: ISO/IEC 19762, ISO/IEC 6523, ISO/IEC 15459, ISO/IEC 17000 and ISO 5157 are the standing sources. Schema-level drift is captured the same way — the casing divergence between lastUpdate and lastUpdated across the EN series is recorded as data, because a term base that cannot state drift cannot support the harmonization annex the workstream owes Part 1.

03 · The concept recordAnatomy of one entry

Each concept is one YAML file in the dataset's concept collection; the register points at the collection and fixes the section taxonomy. The entry for the passport concept itself reads, in full structure:

  • termid: dpp-0001 — a stable dataset identifier, never reused;
  • terms: the designations — the preferred expression "digital product passport" and the preferred abbreviation "DPP", each with normative status;
  • definition: "digital record of product characteristics throughout its life cycle" — verbatim, from EN 18223:2026, clause 3.4, typed authoritative;
  • notes: the source's own note ("example characteristics include environmental sustainability, environmental impact, and recyclability"), carried verbatim with the definition it qualifies;
  • sources: the full provenance list — see below;
  • language_code: eng — the dataset is English-seeded and multilingual by construction.

The provenance list is where the method pays off. For dpp-0001 it holds three entries: EN 18223:2026, 3.4 (authoritative); EN 18239 (prEN 18239:2025), 3.9 (authoritatives-equivalent — a second European authority with the same intension); and ISO/AWI 25534-1 (2026-09-05 draft), 3.1 (conflicting — see section 04). The pattern repeats across the collection: dpp-0002 ("controlled DPP data", section: trust and verification) carries two authoritative sources in agreement (EN 18239 clause 3.1 and EN 18216:2026 clause 3.8); dpp-0003 ("unique product identifier", section: identity and carriers) carries EN 18246 (prEN 18246:2025) clause 3.3 as authoritative with EN 18219:2026 recorded as lineage.

04 · The conflict annotationRecorded, not resolved

The single most consequential entry in the dataset is a source type. The ISO working draft of 25534-1 defines the digital product passport as "a concept or technology which enables sharing of product related information" — a definition in a different genus from the EN series' "digital record of product characteristics throughout its life cycle". The dataset records this as the third source of dpp-0001, typed conflicting, with a modification note quoting the ISO text and stating that the conflict is recorded for harmonization under the proposed Part 1 (Vocabulary).

The design decision worth JTC 5's attention is what the dataset does not do: it does not pre-harmonize. A term base that merged the two definitions would hide the very work the vocabulary part exists to do, and would do so invisibly — no audit trail, no way to know which text won. Recording the conflict as typed data makes harmonization a tracked editorial process: every resolution in the Part 1 draft can be traced to the recorded conflict it resolves, and the EN, UNTP and ISO 59040 correspondences in the harmonization annex are drawn against recorded sources rather than paraphrases.

This is the dataset's answer to the ISO–CEN definitional conflict that anchors workstream 1: the conflict is data, harmonization is process, and the dataset holds both ends.

05 · DeploymentGlossarist as the publishing instrument

The dataset deploys on the glossarist toolchain, in the architecture proven by the cie-eilv dataset: a register.yaml fixing the dataset identity (name dpp, title "Digital Product Passport — Terminology", organization "UniDPP (CalConnect-originated, multi-body stewardship)", status "working dataset") and the eight sections; one YAML file per concept in the concept collection; and lib/ plus scripts/ providing the build per the cie-eilv pattern, with the site configured for glossarist.org/dpp.

Two properties of the deployment matter more than the site itself. First, the Concept Browser edition makes every entry publicly browsable with its citations and conflict annotations — the dataset is inspectable by anyone it proposes to constrain. Second, and load-bearing for the workstream: the Metanorma document for the Part 1 draft is generated from the dataset, not written beside it. The dataset is the single source of truth; the standards document is derived output. Revisions to a definition, a source typing or a conflict resolution are made once, in the concept YAML, and flow to the draft, the browsable site and any future language editions together — the same source-over-derived discipline paper 4 applies to passport renders.

06 · The Part 1 roleDrafting basis for AWI ISO 25534-1

AWI ISO 25534-1 splits into a vocabulary Part 1 and a Part 2 addressing the passport itself (paper 1). This dataset is offered as the drafting basis for Part 1 — the contribution the terminology workstream owes the new work item: a concept base of roughly 80 to 120 entries distilled from the 146 verified concepts, with the register's eight sections available as the clause skeleton of the vocabulary, and a harmonization annex mapping the correspondences EN ↔ UNTP ↔ ISO 59040 ↔ 25534-1 drawn from recorded sources rather than from paraphrase.

Distillation is not deletion. Entries that do not carry into Part 1 remain in the dataset as register content — the browsable collection stays the superset, the standard stays a view — which is the same source-over-derived discipline paper 4 applies to passport renders and paper 2 applies to the semantic registry. A term dropped from a committee draft for scope reasons remains citable, with its provenance, for the delegated acts and sector profiles that will need it next.

The accompanying new-work-item proposal (ISO Form 4) is attached to this contribution under CalConnect attribution, with ISO/TC 154 and the full consortium as supporting bodies — the same sponsorship pattern as the semantic registry NWIP of paper 2.

07 · AvailabilityWhat is open today, what comes next

The dataset is open now: register and concept collection under the UniDPP repositories' Apache-2.0/MIT terms, multi-body stewardship, browsable as a Glossarist Concept Browser edition at glossarist.org/dpp, with the register naming the organization as "UniDPP (CalConnect-originated, multi-body stewardship)" and the status honestly marked "working dataset".

Four next steps are committed, in order: wiring the Metanorma generation of the Part 1 draft into the dataset build so the standard document is always derived; extending the concept collection beyond eng into the languages the deployed services already serve; drift monitoring, so that new prEN revisions and incoming delegated acts land as diffs against recorded sources rather than as silent redefinitions; and, jointly with paper 2's registry requirements, assigning register-grade identifiers to terms under the same ISO 19135 registration discipline proposed for data elements.

The consortium's position, in one sentence: a vocabulary standard for the DPP should be generated from a citable, conflict-annotated term base — and this is that term base, offered as the drafting basis for Part 1 with the work item form attached.
